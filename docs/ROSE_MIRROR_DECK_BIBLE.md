# Rose Mirror Deck Bible

Last updated: 2026-07-10

Rose Mirror is Nilaza's private antique-mirror ritual for love, emotional honesty, self-worth, intimacy, and healthy boundaries. The deck should feel like entering a candlelit room where the user can look at desire, attachment, grief, and choice without being rushed into a prediction.

Core message:

> Love is not disappearing into someone else. Love is learning to see yourself clearly while staying open.

Rose Mirror must never feel like cheap romance, horror occult, deterministic fortune-telling, or a recolor of Moon Garden. It should feel mature, tender, tactile, and premium: a soft boundary ritual for Gen Z users and women who want emotional clarity, not fate claims.

## Audience Promise

- Help users name the feeling underneath the question.
- Help users separate desire, fantasy, fear, and actual need.
- Help users notice reciprocity and emotional labor.
- Help users choose one kind next step without predicting what another person will do.
- Keep every reading framed as symbolic self-reflection and entertainment.

Avoid:

- "They definitely love you" / "This relationship will end" / fixed-fate claims.
- Encouraging testing, surveillance, jealousy, control, or manipulation.
- Overly sexualized romance styling.
- Horror, demonic, gore, threatening occult imagery.
- Generic pink Valentine visuals.

## Visual System

- Format: inner tarot artwork only, generated for the fixed Nilaza card template.
- Final card size: `1024 x 1768px`.
- Inner artwork slot: `656 x 1130px`.
- Primary palette: charcoal black `#151416`, warm ink `#2A2525`, deep rose ink, muted rose `#C9828D`, ivory `#F7F1E6`, champagne gold `#C9A45C`, moon gray `#A8A3A0`.
- Accent restraint: use deep teal or cool moon-blue only as a small counterweight when the scene risks becoming too pink.
- Texture: matte paper, antique glass reflection, candle haze, velvet, silk, dried petals, pearl sheen, soft dust, handwritten letter paper with no readable text.
- Motifs: oval mirrors, hand mirrors, cracked glass, rose petals, thorns, pearls, lockets, keys, ribbons, perfume bottles, water bowls, vanity trays, unopened letters, candle wax.
- Composition: centered symbolic subject or portrait, generous negative space, clear silhouette when reduced on mobile.
- Avoid: text, numbers, card frame inside generated art, watermark, Rider-Waite copy, celebrity likeness, bright neon, horror occult, stock-photo romance, overdecorated one-note pink.

## Story Architecture

Rose Mirror is a journey through a room of emotional reflection:

1. **The Threshold**: the user enters the mirror room and notices the first honest feeling.
2. **The Reflection**: the user sees what they want, what they fear, and where fantasy has softened the truth.
3. **The Crack**: the user meets discomfort, imbalance, attachment, grief, or a boundary they avoided.
4. **The Key**: the user chooses a small action that protects self-worth while keeping compassion.
5. **The Return**: the user leaves with more clarity, not a deterministic answer.

Every card should answer: "What does this card reveal about how the user relates to love, self-worth, or boundaries?"

## Major Arcana Groups

### Threshold And Self-Recognition

Cards: The Fool, The Magician, The High Priestess, The Empress, The Emperor.

Role: establish the user's inner room before focusing on the other person. These cards should show readiness, intuition, self-care, and structure.

Visual rules:

- Use mirrors as doors, vanities, curtains, and first reflections.
- Keep the figure calm and adult; curious but not naive.
- Show self-trust through posture, light, and objects held with care.

Story cues:

- The Fool: stepping into a new emotional room.
- The High Priestess: intuition before proof-seeking.
- The Empress: allowing care without proving worth.
- The Emperor: boundaries as a stable frame.

### Connection And Choice

Cards: The Hierophant, The Lovers, The Chariot, Strength, The Hermit, Wheel of Fortune, Justice.

Role: define how the user connects, chooses, moves, pauses, and balances truth with tenderness.

Visual rules:

- Use pairs of mirrors, paired hands, ribbons, keys, and balanced candlelight.
- Keep intimacy visible but leave breathing room between people or symbols.
- Avoid possessive or overly romantic staging.

Story cues:

- The Lovers: value-aligned choice, not just chemistry.
- Strength: softness with a boundary.
- Justice: truth that is accountable without becoming cold.

### Shadow, Rupture, And Release

Cards: The Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, The Devil, The Tower, The Moon.

Role: show the emotionally difficult cards without fear-based imagery. The user is invited to notice attachment, old patterns, grief, uncertainty, and sudden truth.

Visual rules:

- No horror, no monsters, no gore, no shame.
- Use cracked glass, knotted ribbon, dim candles, fogged mirrors, spilled water, falling petals.
- The scene can be dark, but it must still offer agency.

Story cues:

- The Devil: attachment that can be untied.
- The Tower: truth breaking an old structure, not punishment.
- The Moon: uncertainty that deserves attention but not a final conclusion.

### Repair And Completion

Cards: The Star, The Sun, Judgement, The World.

Role: restore hope, integration, mature joy, and emotional completion.

Visual rules:

- Brighten with ivory and champagne, not neon.
- Use water, open mirrors, loosened ribbons, repaired cracks, circles of petals or pearls.
- The ending should feel peaceful and earned, not triumphant in a loud way.

## Minor Arcana Suit Grammar

### Wands: Desire And Initiative

Emotional territory: chemistry, longing, courage, momentum, tension, the spark to speak or move.

Motifs:

- Candles, match flames, silk ribbons, rose thorns, heat shimmer, diagonal motion.

Design rule:

- Wands can move faster than other suits, but they should stay elegant. Desire must not become sexualized pressure.

### Cups: Attachment And Tenderness

Emotional territory: feeling, mutuality, grief, longing, receiving, emotional nourishment.

Motifs:

- Water cups, perfume bottles, bowls, tear-like glass, paired mirrors, floating petals.

Design rule:

- Cups should feel soft and emotionally readable. Avoid melodrama; show tenderness through small gestures.

### Swords: Truth And Boundaries

Emotional territory: clarity, conflict, difficult speech, clean endings, overthinking, self-protection.

Motifs:

- Silver hairpins, thin blades, cracked glass, cut silk, sharp candlelight, reflected lines.

Design rule:

- Swords should feel precise, not cruel. Avoid violent stabbed-heart cliches where possible.

### Pentacles: Self-Worth And Reciprocity

Emotional territory: value, care, stability, body energy, give-and-receive, practical support.

Motifs:

- Lockets, pearls, keys, coins, velvet boxes, vanity trays, tended roses, folded cloth.

Design rule:

- Pentacles should feel grounded and tactile. Show self-worth as care practices and balanced exchange.

## Court Card Grammar

- Pages: first awareness, emotional learning, careful curiosity.
- Knights: movement, pursuit, momentum that needs direction.
- Queens: embodied care, intuition, warmth with standards.
- Kings: stewardship, maturity, responsible action.

Court cards should feel like adult archetypes, not literal historical royalty. Keep styling contemporary-editorial, mystical, and mature.

## Proof Card Story Notes

### The Fool

Story: a young seeker stands at the threshold of a mirror room. This is not reckless romance; it is the first brave step into emotional honesty.

Required motifs: oval mirror as doorway, candlebeam, petal path, unresolved reflection.

Reject if: the card feels childish, comedic, or like a standard cliff/journey copy.

### The High Priestess

Story: intuition is the quiet before asking for more proof from someone else. The mirror reflects light, not a literal answer.

Required motifs: smoky rose curtains, black mirror, pearls, unreadable notes, crescent or ivory reflection.

Reject if: it looks like a generic witch, fortune-teller, or prophecy scene.

### The Lovers

Story: connection is a choice aligned with values. The two sides nearly meet but keep space.

Required motifs: two mirrors, two hands or reflected hands, untied ribbon, petal ring or breathing room.

Reject if: it becomes a couple portrait, wedding visual, or cheap romance cover.

### Strength

Story: a gentle boundary can be courageous. Softness is not surrender.

Required motifs: hand on cracked mirror, thorned rose branch, silk wrap, warm steady light.

Reject if: it copies lion imagery, turns aggressive, or loses tenderness.

### The Devil

Story: attachment, fantasy, and desire can feel binding, but the knot can be seen and untied.

Required motifs: smoky mirror, too-tight ribbon around a key or wrist, thorns, low candlelight, visible agency.

Reject if: it shows demons, shame, fear, sexualized bondage, or fatalism.

### The Tower

Story: truth breaks an old emotional structure. This is painful clarity, not punishment.

Required motifs: standing mirror cracking, rose-gold fragments, petals blown off a vanity, one candle or object still stable.

Reject if: it copies lightning tower imagery or becomes disaster/horror.

### The Moon

Story: unclear feelings deserve attention, but they are not conclusions yet.

Required motifs: blurred moon in water/perfume, multiple reflections, fog, pearls, soft dark hallway.

Reject if: it feels scary, monstrous, or gives a fake answer.

### Two of Cups

Story: mutuality matters more than one person trying too hard.

Required motifs: two cups, mirrored tray or paired mirrors, reflected hands, equal waterline or balanced candlelight.

Reject if: it becomes marriage/wedding symbolism or one-sided longing.

### Three of Swords

Story: heartache is acknowledged through fabric, glass, and light instead of graphic pain.

Required motifs: three silver pins/hairpins, rose silk, cracked mirror, fallen petals.

Reject if: it uses a pierced heart cliche or horror imagery.

### Six of Pentacles

Story: balanced giving and receiving protects self-worth.

Required motifs: six coins, six pearls, mirrored tray, two hands exchanging, clear symmetry.

Reject if: it looks like charity hierarchy or financial advice.

## Approval Gate

Rose Mirror now has a full 78-card artwork draft by manager request. Treat the complete deck as reviewable draft art, not final approved production art, until it passes all criteria:

- **Emotional specificity:** each card communicates a distinct emotional/boundary moment.
- **Premium texture:** glass, silk, candlelight, paper, petals, and metallic accents feel tactile and refined.
- **Mobile readability:** the core symbol reads clearly when the full card is shown small in the mobile draw/result screens.
- **Motif diversity:** the proof set does not repeat the same two-mirror/petal composition too often.
- **No cheap romance:** no Valentine cliches, wedding tropes, melodramatic stock-romance posing, or over-pink palette.
- **No deterministic fortune-telling:** visual and copy remain reflective; no card implies fixed outcomes.

Approval status terms:

- `approved_for_full_deck`: all criteria pass.
- `revise_before_full_deck`: concept is right but one or more proof cards need rework.
- `full_deck_draft_for_review`: all 78 cards exist and can be reviewed in app/contact sheets, but polish is still expected.
- `full_deck_draft_polish_pass_1`: all 78 cards exist and the first targeted polish batch has reduced template repetition in selected Minor Arcana cards; final art lock still requires full approval.
- `full_deck_draft_polish_pass_2`: all 78 cards exist and two targeted polish batches have improved Wands/Swords, Cups/Pentacles, and court-card hierarchy; final art lock still requires full approval.
- `approved_for_full_deck_app_release`: all 78 cards pass the final app-release art-lock audit; future work should be targeted premium replacement only.
- `blocked`: style direction conflicts with Nilaza positioning or safety guardrails.

Current status: `approved_for_full_deck_app_release`.
